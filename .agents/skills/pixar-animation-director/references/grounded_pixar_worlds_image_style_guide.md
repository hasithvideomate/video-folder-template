# Grounded Animated-Feature Worlds — Universal Image Generation Style Bible

> **Purpose:** A reusable reference for generating ORIGINAL 3D animated-film images that feel like **recognizable everyday reality, art-directed into a beautifully appealing animated world**. The range of reference includes the real-world portions of *Ratatouille*, *Toy Story*, *Up*, *Luca*, *Turning Red*, *Soul*, and *Inside Out*. This is **not** a *Ratatouille*-only or France-only style guide.
>
> **Core distinction:** Familiar places, normal human lives, real furniture, functional streets and believable buildings **with selectively exaggerated design, expressive characters, tactile materials and cinematic lighting**. Neither live-action photorealism nor an invented fantasy metropolis.
>
> **Use:** Attach the full `.md` file as a reference, or paste the **GLOBAL MASTER STYLE INSTRUCTIONS** with a scene description and, optionally, one **WORLD FLAVOR PRESET**. The guide is an independent creative specification, not official Pixar production documentation. Generate original characters, businesses and settings; don't copy recognizable film characters or shot compositions.

---

## 0. Non-negotiable look — highest priority

**Make the world look physically familiar but aesthetically redesigned for high-end character animation.** The environment must convincingly function as a real bedroom, kitchen, street, neighborhood, classroom, market, workshop or city. Its inhabitants must be expressive original 3D-animated people, **not photographs, realistic game characters, 2D illustrations, anime, plastic dolls or exaggerated fantasy beings**.

1. **Believable physical world:** normal gravity, sound construction, functional objects, plausible dimensions, correct perspective, sensible traffic and pedestrian flow.
2. **Artist-designed stylization:** clean readable shapes, slightly softened corners, appealing proportions, intentional silhouettes, restrained asymmetry and carefully simplified form.
3. **World-specific character design:** human characters with personality and a consistent animation style; distinctive face shapes, natural gestures and real clothing, with mild to moderate caricature as the chosen sub-style requires.
4. **Lived-in production design:** dressing communicates a place's purpose, inhabitants, location, history and recent activity. Small props are **positioned logically**, not randomly scattered.
5. **Cinematic image-making:** clear narrative subject, layered depth, controlled light, harmonious colors, soft believable material response and excellent render finish.
6. **Unified styling:** hero characters, extras, furniture, streets and background architecture belong in the **same** designed world. No mix of a cartoon person with a photographic environment.
7. **Normal people, ordinary stories:** the scene can be funny, warm, nostalgic, adventurous or emotional without depending on magic, elemental powers, floating cities or fantasy props.

**Quick shorthand:** `Grounded real-world environment + expressive stylized 3D people + intentional lived-in detail + beautiful animated-feature cinematography.`

## 1. Film-reference spectrum: different worlds, shared principles

These are **high-level art-direction reference points**, not instructions to reconstruct copyrighted scenes. The real-world portions of each film emphasize different kinds of grounded stylization.

| Film reference | Useful real-world influence | Environment treatment | Character / camera treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ratatouille** | Restaurants, food markets, Parisian apartments and streets | Rich functional kitchen detail, weathered masonry, culinary props, inviting pools of light | Characterful adult anatomy; expressive faces; observational staging |
| **Toy Story** | Family homes, bedrooms, yards and suburban streets | Everyday objects with clear readable shapes and convincing scale | Charming designs; sometimes very low, toy-height viewpoints |
| **Up** | Residential streets, porches, living rooms, older homes, travel objects | Nostalgic color and domestic history; strong silhouette and story props | More pronounced **selective** facial and body-shape caricature |
| **Luca** | Italian coastal villages, piazzas, bicycles, cafés | Warm plaster, stone paths, sun-faded paint, handcrafted-feeling simplified geometry | Youthful rounded designs, relaxed seaside composition; real-world scenes only |
| **Turning Red** | Early-2000s Toronto, schools, homes, convenience shops | Everyday city clutter, posters, school supplies, layered community detail | Bouncier faces, lively gestures, slightly stronger stylization and color |
| **Soul** | New York streets, apartments, clubs, music-related spaces | High observational urban detail; textiles, brick, signage, transit and everyday objects | Understated appealing human caricature; nuanced adult mannerisms; real-world scenes only |
| **Inside Out** | Suburban homes, school halls, classrooms and family spaces | Simple, carefully chosen domestic detail supporting clear emotion | Emotion-led framing, clean staging; **not** the abstract mind-world locations |

**Avoid as your default reference:** the fantastical architecture of *Coco*'s Land of the Dead; *Elemental*'s elemental city; dream realms, portals, magical objects and neon-lit impossible structures. A setting can be colorful without becoming fantasy.

### Recommended default blend

For general images, use the **observational environments of *Ratatouille* and *Soul***, the **domestic clarity of *Toy Story* and *Up***, the **warm handcrafted charm of *Luca***, and the **expressive social energy of *Turning Red***. These are qualities to balance, not a request to combine exact existing film imagery.

## 2. Character design specifications — hero and supporting people

### General stylized-human rules

- **Faces:** distinctive head silhouette, readable forehead/cheek/jaw relationships, expressive brows, slightly stylized nose and mouth. Favor intentional asymmetry. Don't give every person the same generic round face.
- **Eyes:** expressive and clearly oriented, but sized to the chosen sub-style; avoid universally huge glossy doll eyes, photographic human eyes, or uncanny glassy gazes.
- **Body:** believable skeletal construction and weight-bearing posture; selectively simplify neck, hands, torso and limb shapes; hands have coherent fingers and physically plausible contact with objects.
- **Skin:** soft subsurface response, subtle color transitions, gentle variation in roughness; not pore-level photographic skin and not perfectly smooth plastic.
- **Hair:** readable sculpted masses and grouped strands, with flyaways kept selective; style reflects personality and place.
- **Clothing:** ordinary region/era-appropriate outfits with designed fabric folds, natural drape, seams, cuffs and subtle wear; avoid costumes unless the job or activity calls for one.
- **Proportions:** use **moderate caricature** by default. An elderly neighbor, teenage student and restaurant server should have different characteristic shapes, not identical proportions.
- **Performance:** every visible person has an emotional beat or ordinary purpose: concentration, tiredness, anticipation, friendly conversation, distraction, hurried work, quiet relaxation.
- **Physical interaction:** feet touch ground, fingers wrap properly around held objects, chairs support seated bodies, bags have visible weight, clothing responds to pose.

### Background characters / extras — explicit generation rules

This section is especially important: **a background crowd should read as a living neighborhood, not a set of duplicate mannequins**.

**Identity variation:** Different facial silhouettes, heights, shoulder widths, builds, approximate ages, hair colors/styles, skin tones, wardrobe layers, footwear and accessories. Design variation must remain consistent with the location, season and period. Diversity should be natural rather than mechanically uniform.

**Silhouette variation:** Mix upright, leaning, seated, walking and turning postures. Vary hats, bags, coat lengths and hairstyle shapes. Avoid equally spaced figures all the same height.

**Action variation:** Assign specific mundane micro-actions and supporting props. Examples:
- Café: reading a menu, carrying two cups, wiping a table, leaning closer to a friend, glancing toward the pastry case.
- Street: pushing a stroller, locking a bicycle, looking at a shop window, carrying groceries, waiting at a crossing.
- School: adjusting a backpack, searching a locker, talking in a pair, finishing a snack, checking a noticeboard.
- Home: sorting laundry, reaching for a cabinet, leaning over homework, watering a plant, setting a table.
- Market: selecting produce, arranging crates, folding a bag, exchanging change, speaking with a vendor.
- Music club: moving a chair, adjusting an instrument case, listening to the stage, chatting quietly at a table.

**Natural grouping:** People have reasons to stand near one another. Form small pairs, families, queues, customers and staff; preserve believable interpersonal distances, walking routes and eye contact. A crowd should have social structure, not evenly distributed dots.

**Correct perspective and image hierarchy:**

| Depth | Character treatment | Detail / posing expectation |
|---|---|---|
| **Foreground extra** | Distinct but secondary to the main subject | Readable action, original face, clothing folds, plausible hand-to-prop contact |
| **Midground extra** | Clear silhouette and role | Simple expression, distinct outfit color and body shape, believable activity |
| **Far background extra** | Simplified coherent figure | Very little facial detail, broad readable pose and silhouette, no oversized eyes or giant heads |

- **Density:** match the setting: 0–2 people in a quiet room; a handful in a small shop; several natural groups on a lively street. Don't force a large crowd into every frame.
- **Staging:** extras should never obscure a hero face, important gesture or storytelling object. Leave a clear route around the scene's focal point.
- **Gaze:** most extras look at the person or object relevant to their activity, **not straight into the camera**.
- **Continuity across a series:** keep fixed outfit/face descriptors for recurring extras, not just the lead character.
- **Failure modes to explicitly forbid:** duplicated faces, cloned clothing, synchronized walking, floating feet, intersecting bodies, bizarre elbows/hands, wrong perspective scale, people standing through tables, unnatural crowd lines.

### Reusable identity cards

Keep this record for each recurring character in a multi-image series:


## 3. Architecture and world design: recognizable locations first

### Core physical rules

- Buildings and rooms should resemble architecture that **could be built and lived in**: credible rooflines, window placement, railings, stairs, doors, utility pipes, rainwater drainage and material thickness.
- Respect region, climate and time period. An Italian village, Canadian school and New York shopfront should not share interchangeable facade details.
- Familiar proportions first, gentle exaggeration second. A curved doorframe, slightly generous window or charmingly uneven roof may help the design—but avoid melted structures or impossible suspended buildings.
- Give spaces an internal layout: entrances, exits, accessible routes, storage, work areas, activity zones and places where people gather.
- Design with **large shape → medium structure → small dressing**. Don't make windowsills and signs before the street perspective works.
- Build three layers: meaningful foreground, playable midground, atmospheric background. The environment should read immediately even when viewed as a thumbnail.

### Useful everyday location types and typical details

| Location | Architecture / main fixtures | Motivated background details |
|---|---|---|
| **Family bedroom** | Bed, desk, window, closet, shelves | Books beside a lamp, shoes by the door, posters, laundry basket, schoolbag, toys from a recent activity |
| **Kitchen / dining room** | Counters, stove, sink, cupboards, table | Cutlery by plates, groceries near unpacking area, dish towel, ingredients on a prep board, small crumbs |
| **Apartment living room** | Couch, side table, window, shelving | Family photos, well-used cushions, remote by seating area, plants at a sunny window, mail by the entry |
| **Café / restaurant** | Customer route, tables, service station, kitchen access | Menus, cups, bread baskets, stack of saucers, coats, receipts, waiter trays, believable staff motion |
| **Bookshop / corner store** | Shelves, counter, display, clear aisles | Price tags, reused boxes, checkout items, bagging station, slightly varied stacked products |
| **School / classroom** | Desks, noticeboards, lockers, corridors | Class projects, notebooks, backpacks, posters, student art, pencils and schedules |
| **City sidewalk** | Curbs, drainage, crossings, storefronts, doors | Delivery crates by service entrances, bicycles at racks, shop signs, window displays, trash bins in sensible places |
| **Coastal village** | Plastered houses, shutters, piazza, stone streets | Laundry, simple market displays, bicycles, terracotta planters, weathered paint, signs suited to the region |
| **Music-related room** | Stage, stand, chairs, cases, acoustic wall materials | Sheet music, stands, instrument cases, cables where plausible, drink glasses away from electronics |
| **Suburban porch / yard** | Steps, porch rail, walkway, mailbox, garden | Flowerpots, garden tools, mail, worn furniture, a hose, leaves collected at edges |

### Build environmental history

Small details should imply time: slightly shiny armrests, faded sign paint, a frequently used cutting board, mismatched dishes, a repaired chair, old photographs, seasonally appropriate coats, or a bicycle with real wear. Add imperfections **where physical contact or weather logically produces them**, not a global damage filter.

## 4. Set dressing and background details — exact positioning logic

**Rule: Every important prop should answer at least one of these questions:** Who uses it? For what? Why is it here? What just happened? What does it tell us about the place?

### Five passes of environmental dressing

1. **Functional structure:** doors, counters, shelving, tables, cabinets, street furniture, circulation routes.
2. **Operational items:** objects needed for activities: cookware near stoves, books near study areas, shopping baskets near checkout.
3. **Activity traces:** unfinished tasks and story clues: an open notebook, half-drunk tea, groceries not yet unpacked, a pushed-back chair.
4. **Personal identity and age:** family photographs, favorite mug, old paint, subtle repaired parts, regional signage, neighborhood culture.
5. **Atmosphere:** appropriate daylight, soft window reflections, steam near hot food, rain streaks, breeze in a curtain, foliage movement.

### Cluster, don't scatter

Create meaningful groups of **one main object and two to five supporting objects**:

- **Homework cluster:** open workbook + pencil + eraser + school bag by the chair.
- **Morning coffee cluster:** mug + teaspoon + folded newspaper + plate with toast crumbs.
- **Cooking cluster:** pot + utensil + heat source + chopped ingredients + towel.
- **Music cluster:** instrument case + sheet music + folding chair + coat nearby.
- **Street delivery cluster:** stacked crates + hand trolley + receipt clipboard beside the appropriate shop.
- **Seaside window cluster:** half-open shutters + planter + weathered paint + laundry line located where it could attach.

Choose deliberate spacing and slight irregularity. Don't align every prop to a grid and don't place the same decorative items in every corner.

### Focal detail vs. supporting detail

- **Hero-zone objects:** distinctive, high quality, intentionally placed, clearly visible and motivated by the action.
- **Near supporting objects:** several coherent clusters; good shape design, material variation and contact shadows.
- **Midground objects:** enough to establish how people use the space; reduced contrast and small-detail complexity.
- **Far background objects:** broad recognizable forms; repetition with limited variation is acceptable; avoid competing with the focal point.
- **Negative space:** preserve uncluttered surfaces and readable background immediately behind a main face, gesture or emotional prop.

### Population and clutter control

A crowded market can have many details but still needs organized pathways, clean silhouettes and zones of rest. A bedroom should reflect its owner without having every possible possession visible. **Density must follow location, story and camera angle**; there is no universal object count.

## 5. Asset language and material finish

### Modeling / shape language

- Favor readable broad curves and generously clear silhouettes.
- Bevel appropriate hard edges so they catch soft highlights. Do not make all objects marshmallow-like.
- Retain real construction details: seam lines, material thickness, joints, hinges, furniture legs, window reveals, handles.
- Vary repeated assets in scale **plausibly**, orientation, color, type and context. Identical windows may repeat in architecture, but curtains, lighting and nearby details can vary.
- Simplify tiny geometry whose detail would not read at final image size.

### Materials

- **Wood:** controlled grain, moderately soft reflection, selectively worn touch points.
- **Metal:** differentiated steel, brass, copper and painted metal with appropriate roughness and broad glints.
- **Fabric:** designed folds, readable weave without excessive microtexture, correct softness and thickness.
- **Skin:** stylized soft scattering, warm transitions, natural variation without hyper-detailed pores.
- **Glass:** believable thickness, reflections and transparency; avoid empty black windows or perfect mirrors everywhere.
- **Paint/plaster/brick:** broad color variation and regional weathering, not identical procedural noise everywhere.
- **Food:** recognizable form and tactile surface response; subtly appetizing exaggeration, not synthetic plastic.

**Rendering direction:** premium 3D feature-film image; nuanced bounce light, elegant specular highlights, soft contact shadows, restrained roughness variation, coherent textures and polished antialiasing. Not a game screenshot; not photorealistic product photography.

## 6. Cinematic camera, color and lighting

### Camera and composition

- State the **story beat** and focal subject before requesting environmental detail.
- Compose in foreground, midground and background; use partial overlaps for depth.
- Choose camera height to communicate viewpoint: adult eye level for human social scenes; low near-floor view for a child's toy-scale perspective; seated view for an intimate home scene.
- Use slightly wide or naturalistic perspective where it serves the environment. Keep vertical architectural lines plausible unless artistic exaggeration is intentional.
- Use depth of field selectively: backgrounds may be softened, but enough must remain readable to establish the place.
- Position background characters **around** the main action instead of directly behind a protagonist's head.

### Color and light

- Begin with a believable time of day and real motivated sources: window sun, sky, overhead fixture, café lamp, streetlamp, cloudy daylight.
- Use warm/cool contrast thoughtfully: warm interior against cool rainy sidewalk, warm afternoon sun with cooler shadow bounce, or soft neutral daylight in a school.
- Color palettes can be playful, nostalgic or lively **without full-frame neon oversaturation**.
- Allow subtly richer saturation for a chosen energetic sub-style (e.g., early-2000s school drama) than for a subdued adult urban drama.
- Keep key subject clear through value grouping, material contrast and lighting—not through artificial outlines or glows around every figure.
- Retain texture and color in both highlights and shadows. Contact shadows anchor furniture, hands and feet.

## 7. GLOBAL MASTER STYLE INSTRUCTIONS — copy into image generation

Use this block **in every prompt**; then add a specific scene and optionally a preset from Section 8.

```text
STYLE TARGET — GROUNDED, CHARACTER-DRIVEN, HIGH-END 3D ANIMATED FEATURE:
Create an ORIGINAL cinematic frame of a recognizable everyday place in the real world, beautifully redesigned through the visual language of polished character animation. The location should be architecturally believable, functional, region-appropriate and physically coherent: a real home, street, café, school, store, neighborhood, studio or workshop. Maintain ordinary gravity, normal utilities, plausible furniture and correct object scale.

STYLIZATION:
Appealing sculpted geometry; selectively softened edges; clear shapes and silhouettes; warm, expressive human faces with varied proportions and subtle-to-moderate caricature; nuanced expressions, believable anatomy, textured but non-photographic skin, attractive grouped hair and naturally draping real-world clothing. Consistent animation design across all humans, animals, objects and architecture. Neither literal photorealism nor plastic toy-like caricature.

ENVIRONMENT & SET DRESSING:
A beautifully observed lived-in place, with detailed yet logical background dressing. Furnish the environment according to its real function. Arrange small props in believable activity-based clusters, with signs of age and everyday use. Keep walking paths open and the main action readable. Build depth from meaningful foreground props, active midground and quieter atmospheric background. Rich detail but not uniform clutter or decorative noise.

BACKGROUND PEOPLE:
Original, diverse, visually distinct supporting characters consistent with the region, era and setting. Different facial silhouettes, ages, builds, clothing, hairstyles and heights; plausible feet-on-ground perspective and natural proportions. Each extra does an ordinary location-specific micro-action, individually or in small social groups, rather than staring at camera. Avoid cloned extras. Background characters support rather than overpower the main subject.

CINEMATOGRAPHY & FINISH:
One clear story moment and focal subject, directed camera perspective, strong composition, layered depth, appealing palette, believable practical or daylight lighting, natural bounce illumination, controlled soft reflections, tactile real materials, refined shadow contact, filmic tonal response and exceptionally clean 3D animated-feature rendering.

DO NOT MAKE:
A fantasy world, magical city, elemental architecture, impossible floating buildings, neon cyberpunk metropolis, surreal dream-space environment, anime, 2D illustration, live-action photo, generic realistic game cutscene, shiny plastic-doll render, cluttered incoherent layout, cloned crowds, or recognizable copied film characters.
```

- [ ] Are all people consistently stylized, with distinct faces, outfits, silhouettes and ages?
- [ ] Does every visible background person have a believable action and spatial relationship?
- [ ] Are hands, feet, seating, weight, scale, line of sight and interactions physically coherent?
- [ ] Do buildings and furniture obey real physical construction rules?
- [ ] Can the viewer immediately identify the main storytelling subject?
- [ ] Are small objects organized in understandable functional clusters?
- [ ] Are high-detail areas reserved for the subject and relevant nearby objects?
- [ ] Is there enough empty space to keep the image legible?
- [ ] Are material types distinguishable and pleasantly tactile rather than plastic?
- [ ] Does motivated cinematic lighting support the emotion?
- [ ] Do the original designs avoid recognizable copies of existing movie scenes or characters?
- [ ] Is the world free from unwanted magical/fantasy architecture?

### Troubleshooting

| Problem | Correction to append |
|---|---|
| **Too much like a photo** | “Distinctly sculpted, designed 3D animated-feature humans and gently simplified geometry; non-photographic facial planes.” |
| **Too cartoony / childish** | “Believable human anatomy, moderate selective exaggeration, normal architecture, natural material response.” |
| **Too much like *Ratatouille* specifically** | “Use the chosen region and world preset; remove Parisian architecture and food themes unless requested.” |
| **Environment too empty** | “Add five motivated prop clusters tied to occupants' activities, without blocking movement or faces.” |
| **Environment too cluttered** | “Keep only story-supporting objects; group props by task; preserve quieter background and clear floor routes.” |
| **People duplicated** | “Give each visible extra a distinct silhouette, age, hair, outfit, height, posture and micro-action.” |
| **Photographic extras with cartoon hero** | “Render every person in one coherent 3D animated-feature character design system.” |
| **Wrong regional feel** | “Honor the specified architecture, climate, signage conventions, dress and historical period.” |
| **Too fantasy / neon** | “Only ordinary real-world materials, structures and civic infrastructure; grounded colors and practical lighting.” |
| **No narrative focus** | “Put one meaningful gesture in the foreground, frame it with secondary activity and reduce competing contrast.” |

## Final rule

**Imagine a place that actually could exist, populated by people who genuinely live there. Then art-direct the shapes, faces, textures, color, staging and light until it feels like a lovingly crafted animated feature—not a literal photograph, a caricature overload, or an invented fantasy universe.**
