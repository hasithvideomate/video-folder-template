# Automatic UGC cuts and pacing

Use this stage for generated UGC and supplied talking-head A-roll after source/dialogue verification. Quality review of each generated take starts immediately after its own download. Pacing cleanup is a separate editing stage: a good generated take may still contain long pre-roll, post-roll or pauses. Do not deliver a straight concatenation with those pauses intact.

The user wants speech that flows clearly between cuts. Automatically perform the steps below within the existing editing authorization; do not ask for confirmation per cut. Preserve every approved word, language, phrase order and ending. Keep original media untouched. Edit linked picture and sound together in the existing Premiere project, with fixed framing and no added text.

## Default pace

Read `File/pacing_policy.json`. These are starting values for natural, fairly tight UGC, not proof that an interval is safe to remove:

- Inspect pauses longer than 0.50 seconds. For unnecessary within-phrase pauses, aim for about 0.24 seconds; at sentence boundaries aim for about 0.40 seconds. Keep a longer pause when meaning, a breath, emphasis or a meaningful gesture needs it.
- Remove dead air before the first word and after the last word of each take. Start with 0.08 seconds before speech and 0.12 seconds after speech, expanding as necessary to preserve soft consonants, breath, reverb tails and gesture completion. Preserve about 0.25 seconds of natural hold after the final ending where appropriate.
- At a join, judge the **combined** outgoing tail, incoming lead and any timeline gap. Target roughly 0.24 seconds between continuing phrases or 0.40 seconds between sentences. Do not retain a full target pause on both sides and accidentally double it. Existing source pauses count toward the target.
- Detect low-level regions initially at -40 dBFS for at least 0.35 seconds. Calibrate against the actual recording. This identifies candidates only: quiet speech, unvoiced consonants and breaths can be below a threshold. Silence detection is never sufficient authority to delete an interval.
- Remove unintended empty timeline gaps. An empty video-track interval may contain intentional B-roll, another speaker, music or audio on another track; inspect all relevant tracks before collapsing time.

Do not speed up the speaker to fix pauses. Do not add zooms to hide jump cuts. Preserve gesture meaning and full-hand framing; choose a nearby quieter hand pose or preserve a little extra time when a cut would interrupt the phrase's gesture. Use suitable supplied B-roll only within the established workflow.

## Execute with the installed Premiere tools

1. Discover operations with a bounded `get_capabilities` query; verify the selected bridge and inspect the actual saved project, sequence, frame rate, source items, linked A/V, track locks and sync locks. Do not treat discovery as a live connection. The master template has no editable source media.
2. Obtain actual word timing from the recording. Use supported Premiere transcription and source revisions; verify words against the approved script and listen to ambiguous boundaries. `plan_pause_tightening` currently requires `word_timeline` with the exact source project-item ID, the revision returned by `get_clip_transcript_uxp`, and ordered source-time words. If that transcript is unavailable on the selected bridge, report it and use supported local audio/transcription evidence to prepare candidates. Never invent a Premiere transcript revision or silently switch backends.
3. With a valid transcript, call `plan_pause_tightening` using the policy's `pause_planner` values and the actual supported frame rate. The result is a local proposal, not applied cuts. Use `detect_silence` on actual media to inspect potential dead air and edge handles; it returns **source** ranges. Cross-check with word timing and listening. Protected meaning, breaths and gestures override numeric targets.
4. Inspect every clip edge and assembled clip join, including joins between separately generated clips. A per-source pause planner cannot see the combined tail/lead at a cross-file boundary. Record exact source in/out choices and the intended residual pause per join in `File/edit_notes.md` or a versioned edit-decision JSON. Distinguish source seconds from timeline seconds.
5. Map chosen source removals with `map_source_ranges_to_timeline` on the correct inspected track and source item. Follow pagination and verify the mapping; speed-changed/reversed sources are refused by that mapper. Never apply source offsets directly as timeline offsets. Separately page through `get_timeline_gaps` with a minimum small enough to include single-frame holes and inspect the relevant tracks.
6. Prefer the supported guarded preview/apply route for the selected edits. The currently registered `ripple_remove_timeline_ranges` is experimental, supports integer frame rates only, and accepts at most 50 sorted, non-overlapping timeline intervals. Do not round 29.97/23.976 to an integer to use it. For unsupported timebases, use another actually supported typed route or a reversible source-range assembly; do not fake support.
7. For that ripple route, first use `dry_run: true`, `range_content: delete`, `scope: sync_locked`, the actual active sequence ID and project guard. Inspect all participating tracks and previewed effects on A/V. Apply the identical reviewed ranges once with `dry_run: false` and its exact single-use token under the user's existing edit authorization. Do not unlock unrelated tracks or change sync locks silently. On large plans, apply bounded groups and remap against fresh state before each next group; offsets shift after ripple removal. Never replay a consumed token after an unknown outcome.
8. Read back clip ranges, ordering, A/V alignment, durations and remaining gaps after edits. Listen across **every** join at normal speed, with context on both sides, and watch the associated mouth/hand motion. Use short audio fades only where supported and needed for clicks, without fading away words or overlapping dialogue. Correct clipped consonants, doubled breaths, awkward gesture jumps, abrupt endings and pauses that still feel empty.
9. Review the complete assembled sequence for clear phrase flow and the preserved ending. Automatically delegate an independent pacing review using actual sequence playback or a concrete review artifact when supported. Review renders still need the existing draft-render authorization; do not produce a final export to get around that boundary. The reviewer checks all joins, required wording, breaths, cadence, gestures, gaps, A/V sync and the no-caption/no-zoom rules. Missing listening/playback evidence stays uncertain. Mark pacing complete only after actual verification; source-clip review is not assembled-edit review.

## Handoff and limits

The batch runner adds a mandatory `autoCut` contract to its edit handoff, including a fingerprint of the current policy and `status: pending`. `ready` means source clips passed review and are ready to edit; `editingComplete: false` explicitly means the timeline has not been cut yet. The agent carries out this stage through Premiere, then records the actual sequence/version, source/timeline ranges, time removed, protected exceptions and review evidence in project memory/edit notes. Do not modify old generation reviews to claim the edited sequence was reviewed.

An unreachable Premiere bridge blocks live timeline edits, not template upkeep or local evidence preparation. State that limitation plainly. Restore the selected bridge, inspect the intended project again, and continue from fresh state. Final-version export approval remains unchanged.
